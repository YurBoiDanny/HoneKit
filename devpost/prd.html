The core journey

The demo person is both a camera operator and the Camera team lead, so the demo moves between the two sides without losing their bag. Step through it — each stage shows what they see, what they do, the state it lives in, and the criterion that proves it works.

Two sides, one bag

You sit on both sides of this. The member side proves a bag can fill from earned competency; the leader side proves one big bag can read the whole crew's health.

The member loop

Your bag, filling

  • Own bag starts empty.
  • Answer the Camera deck — fill-in-the-blank and a framing scenario.
  • Right answer → the bag reacts; wrong → not quite, try again, answer shown, card returns later.
  • Finish the deck → a tool drops in when you return home, plain or honed.
  • Personalize — colour and style. Peek at a teammate's.

The two card types

Only two in the POC. Both are authored, so both always have a defined right answer.

Image-based fill-in-the-blank

An image, a sentence with a missing word (jargon or gear), and a short set of word choices you tap to fill the blank. No free text, no fuzzy matching — the author supplies the choices, so there is always exactly one right answer.

You see
Gear photo · sentence with a blank · tappable word chips.
You do

Right
The bag reacts — an item nudges in and back.
Wrong
“Not quite — try again.” Answer revealed (default; the person can switch it off with the gear), with an explanation of the right answer and why the chosen one is wrong. The card returns later.

Framing scenario

A photo with a draggable, resizable frame. You position the crop for the situation — “leader teaching at the lectern” — and submit. Correctness is judged by how close your frame sits to the author's target frame, in both position and size. Inside a tolerance, it's correct. Tolerance is a build-time dial

The bag is the health

Health is never a chart. A well-kept crew shows a shiny, packed bag; a quiet crew shows one worn and poorly kept. The POC seeds the worn bag rather than simulating decay.

Empty

First use. Inviting, not broken.

Filled & honed

A tool lands plain or honed, by how well you ran it.

Worn

An inactive member. Seeded so the contrast shows in one glance.

Team bag — the aggregate

One big bag per team. Each tool's fill is the share of that skill's lessons completed across every member of the role — total completions ÷ (members × lessons) — so ten camera crew with four finished reads 40%. The filled part is solid and in colour, the rest an empty outline. In the demo the Camera bag sits about half filled.

Personalization

Colour + bag style (backpack, duffel, sling). Rewards and special designs from leaders are deferred. A teammate's view shows your choices — that's the show-off half.

States and boundaries

What the person sees

  • First use — the bag is empty; the emptiness is inviting.
  • Empty state — a teammate with an empty bag shows an empty bag, not an error.
  • Wrong answer — “Not quite — try again,” answer revealed, card queued to return.
  • Deck complete / early exit — both reach the score / pass result.
  • Worn bag — an inactive member's bag renders worn and dull.

Boundaries

  • Persistence — progress and bag survive closing the app.
  • Read-only — leader views and a teammate's bag are viewable, not editable.
  • No auth — seeded members stand in for accounts.
  • Reveal defaults on — a per-person setting via the gear, not a per-deck one.
  • Explanations — the right answer explains itself; the picked wrong choice says why it's wrong.
  • Two or three service seams — enough for a genuine distribution.

Building now / deferred

Deck authoring, spaced repetition, and the honed finish were added during this interview and are kept deliberately. The dashboard strip and the extra card types were cut.

Building

  • One role with content: Camera.
  • Member loop: empty bag → two card types → right/wrong feedback with per-card explanations → reveal toggle → spaced repetition → score / pass.
  • The bag: fills per completed deck; tool lands on return home; plain vs honed; colour + style; teammate view. “At level” = lessons completed; the team bag scales that across the role.
  • Leader: team bag as aggregate health; bag wall; roster; team-bag ↔ own-bag switch.
  • Deck authoring: create, add the two card types (incl. a framing target and per-card explanations), assign.
  • Seeded members, including one inactive with a worn bag.
  • Persistence between sessions.
  • A responsive interface for phone and desktop.
  • Meeting plan: upload a brief, parse it into editable requirements, run three strategies, override by hand with warnings, save the plan, turn gaps into assignments.
  • Five real service boundaries: Content, Progress, Roster & Bags, Requirements, Recommendations.

Deferred

  • Dashboard strip — the leader analytics service; the team bag already says it in a glance.
  • A separate health bar — redundant with the team bag.
  • Multiple choice, reorder/checklist, audio melody ID, terminal scenarios.
  • Content for video directing, multimedia, audio, networking.
  • Real accounts and registration.
  • Live bag wear-and-tear over time — seeded instead.
  • Richer customization, leader rewards, badge tooling, the leader bag.
  • Comments on decks — surfaced during the interview, not in scope.
  • Discord / WhatsApp notifications.
  • A service per feature.

Meeting plan added in 4-spec

The leader already receives a requirements document for every meeting they serve. This turns that document into a roster and a training plan — and closes the loop the team bag otherwise leaves open: a diagnosis with nothing to do about it.

The flow

  1. Upload the brief — .txt, .md or .pdf.
  2. A model returns skills, minimum level, headcount, purpose and criticality.
  3. The leader edits it. Everything is editable, because the parse is a draft.
  4. Recommendations run over confirmed requirements and current progress.
  5. The leader picks a strategy and can override by hand.
  6. The saved plan's gaps become an assignment.

What the model may and may not do

It parses. It never ranks people, never decides a roster, and never invents a skill — the role's catalogue is passed with the request, and anything outside it fails validation.

A real brief asks for PA, streaming and audio recording too. Those are listed as outside this role rather than quietly mapped to something that sounds close.

Ranking, warnings and strategy are deterministic rules over progress data, so every suggestion can say why and the demo repeats exactly. If the model is unavailable, the manual form is the fallback — and it had to exist anyway.

Critical

Maximise coverage of required skills. For a service that cannot afford a miss.

Balanced

Pair the strongest member on a skill with someone below level on it. Grows the crew during the meeting.

Develop

Prefer members below level; the meeting is the training. Readiness drops, and the screen says so.

And it never ranks people. The crew is never ordered by percentage in a way that reads as a leaderboard — suggestions are ordered by fit to this meeting, with reasons shown. Comparison stays a doorway to mentoring, per the non-goals.

Non-goals

  • No scoring or ranking people against each other — comparison encourages, never demoralizes.
  • Not a general-purpose study app — the bag is the kernel.
  • Not building the future services now.
