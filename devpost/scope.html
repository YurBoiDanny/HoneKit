The unique kernel

The bag. It starts empty. Every card you complete puts something visible inside it. Competency becomes something you can see — and share.

Nothing yet

A new member opens the app to an empty bag.

Earned

Remove the bag and this is a generic quiz app with a leaderboard. Keep it, and comparison stops being a scoreboard and becomes “how'd you get that?” — a doorway to mentorship and celebration.

You come back because a bag is never finished. For a leader, the loop is different: open the team bag and see at a glance how much of the team is genuinely proficient.

Who it's for

A camera operator

Concretely, you. They serve Sunday services, but also midweek meetings, Saturdays, and the occasional special or extra event.

They need proficiency in a primary and a secondary role so a leader can call on them flexibly — and today there's no way to see their own growth, or learn from a peer, except by showing up.

A team leader

Serves on the team like everyone else, so they carry their own member bag.

But they also grow people, and need a read on where the team actually is — especially with thin crews and events that appear midweek.

You are both. A camera operator and a team lead — which is why the proof of concept demos both sides, not just the member's.

What “working” looks like

The demo — both sides

As a member: a member opens the app (seeded profile) and sees an empty bag. They get a camera foundational deck and answer two cards: an image-based fill-in-the-blank on jargon, and a framing scenario — positioning the crop for “leader teaching at the lectern.” The bag visibly gains its first tools and patches. They open a teammate's bag and see theirs.

As a team lead: they open the bag wall — the crew's bags side by side — and can step back to the team bag, one big bag for the whole camera team whose camera is only half filled: solid on one side, bare outline on the other.

One person is both, so the demo moves between the two without losing their own bag.

The “oh, that's cool” beat: the empty bag filling — a tool landing in it the moment you get an answer right.

The leader's view

Two ways to look at the same truth — each member's own bag, or the team as one. The leader chooses.

The default view: who's got what, at a glance — and who to go ask. Each bag is personal to its owner; the leader sees the collection, not anyone's private detail.

Step back and the team becomes one aggregate bag per role, where each tool fills in proportion to how many people are actually at level.

If only about half the camera team is proficient, the camera is half filled — solid colour and detail on one side, bare outlined border on the other, at a glance.

Half-filled camera = half the crew at level

The filled half is solid and in colour; the rest is transparent, outlined only. Want the detail? Click through for the plainer list view. Illustration is an example, not a metric.

Bag flavor

Personalization means the bag is yours: colors, bag style, glossy or matte patches and pins, a drawing on the front. An illustrative palette, not a spec — the point is that it becomes something you want to show off.

Bag style Glossy / matte Pin & stripe shades Your drawing

Richer customization lands later. The PoC needs enough flavor that the bag feels like yours.

The boundary

In the proof of concept Now
  • One role: Camera. Video directing is a listed team/role, but with no content yet.
  • Both sides demoed: a team member's loop, and a team lead's bag wall and team bag — the same person is both.
  • Two card types: image-based fill-in-the-blank (jargon, gear) and a framing scenario (position the shot for a given situation).
  • The bag: empty → fills with tools/patches → bag flavor → view a teammate's bag.
  • Leader view, two modes the leader switches between: the bag wall (members' bags side by side) and the team bag (one aggregate bag per role, tools filled in proportion to team proficiency), with a plainer list view on click. Read-only.
  • Two or three real service boundaries, so the orchestration and microservice goals have a genuine seam to work with. Architecture decided in 4-spec.
  • Seeded members instead of real accounts.
Later · with a seam left where it's cheap Later
  • Content for video directing, multimedia, audio, networking
  • More card types: ordering/checklist, audio melody ID, terminal scenarios
  • Deck authoring and targeted assignment (“push this to the people who need it”)
  • Badge design tooling; the leader bag; multiple / role-shaded bags
  • Richer bag customization (custom drawings, more styles and finishes)
  • Discord notifications (a seam in the PoC), then WhatsApp
  • Real accounts and registration
Future services · planned, not built Future

They belong in their own services because they tie back into training — named here so the PoC's boundaries leave room for them.

  • Leader analytics → rostering, scheduling, inventory: who's best for the job, what each team needs, training plans, who to schedule. Feeds off the same progress data the bag uses.
  • Chat
  • Notifications (Discord / WhatsApp)
Explicitly cut from the PoC Cut

Nothing is cut from the product vision — the above is sequencing. Cut from the PoC:

  • Real authentication and registration. Seeded members prove the same thing in the demo; auth is a timesink that says nothing about the kernel.
  • Building any future service now. They're named so we plan for them, not so we build them this weekend.
  • A service per feature. Two or three seams chosen so the distribution is justified — not a dozen services holding nothing.

Learning goals this must serve

Multi-agent orchestration

You drive the coordination of the agents — not the agent.

Small-scale microservices

A distributed shape small enough to hold in your head, with real seams.

Both goals want more surface, not less. So the PoC is cut to keep real seams rather than removed surface. 4-spec picks the boundaries.
