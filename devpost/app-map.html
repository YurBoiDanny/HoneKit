HoneKit

A flashcard app for the camera crew where what you prove becomes tools and patches in a bag you carry. Five small services, one React app, one deployment model.

The shape

Contentskills, lessons, decks, cards, authored targets
Progresssessions, grading, requeues, completions
Roster & Bagspeople, bags, the wall, derived tools
RequirementsPDF/text → model → editable requirements
Recommendationsdeterministic rosters, gaps, saved plans
FrontendReact + Vite in dev, nginx when deployed

The tool is never stored: Roster computes it from Progress completions every time the bag is read.

Follow one action

Answer a framing card and watch the tool arrive. Three stops, real symbols.

  1. Where you answer. src/frontend/src/screens/DeckScreen.tsx — answerWith posts the attempt; advance asks Progress for the next card. The canvas itself is src/frontend/src/components/FramingCard.tsx and FramingCanvas.tsx (normalised 0–1 rectangle).
  2. Where it is judged. src/services/Progress/Endpoints/ProgressEndpoints.cs — MapPost("/sessions/{id}/attempts"…) calls Domain/FramingRules.cs (IoU ≥ 0.70) or Domain/SequenceRules.cs (ordering/PTZ), records the attempt and the requeue. On finish, ProgressDomain.Evaluate computes first-try, pass and honed.
  3. Where it becomes gear. src/services/Roster/Endpoints/RosterEndpoints.cs — MapGet("/members/{id}/bag"…) joins completions to Content lessons through Domain/BagRules.cs; src/frontend/src/components/BagArt.tsx draws the patch, with a drop class until the acknowledgement is posted.
A second route: the brief becomes a roster
  1. src/services/Requirements/Parsing/RequirementParser.cs — text/PDF → IChatClient → validated set; Endpoints/RequirementEndpoints.cs stores it before you edit.
  2. src/services/Recommendations/Domain/RecommendationRules.cs — deterministic ordering, gaps, sustain; Endpoints/RecommendationEndpoints.cs saves plans.
  3. src/frontend/src/components/Recommendations.tsx — strategy switch, hand-pick warnings, gap assignment.

To change this, start here

  • A new card type: Content/Data/ContentSeed.cs and CardDto → grading in Progress/Domain + ProgressEndpoints → a branch in DeckScreen.tsx → the authoring form in BuildScreen.tsx.
  • The AI reader: non-secrete defaults live in src/HoneKit.AppHost/appsettings.json under Parameters; the client, timeout and prompt live in RequirementParser.cs; the status sentence is Domain/AiStatusRules.cs.
  • A skill or its spread: ContentSeed.cs (skills/lessons/cards) and ProgressSeed.cs (who completed what), then dotnet test HoneKit.slnx.
  • The bag's look: src/frontend/src/components/BagArt.tsx for the drawing and patch labels; tokens.css for colour; BagScreen.tsx/TeamBagScreen.tsx for layout.

A practice to reuse

Test the dependency before the app, and measure the boundary — not just the happy path.

The live reader failed twice for different reasons. A direct curl to OpenCode Go returned 400 MissingSessionID; the app was hiding that behind “the reader did not answer”, so the fix was to send x-opencode-session (73a5be6). The second failure was ours: a 30-second timeout, while the same brief took 19.8 s and 28.1 s. One warm success proved nothing about the boundary; timing two runs exposed it, and the budget became 90 s (653b05e).

Next time an integration misbehaves, reproduce it against the provider with the smallest possible request, read the provider's exact error, then time the real payload. That distinguishes “the provider is down” from “our wrapper is wrong” from “our deadline is too tight”.
